How the detection works

Three steps run continuously, roughly 10 times a second, entirely on your device.

01 — Capture

Read the face

A lightweight neural network locates your face in each webcam frame and maps 68 landmark points across it, including six points around each eye.

02 — Measure

Score eye openness

The eye landmarks are reduced to a single number — the eye aspect ratio — which drops sharply when the eyelids close and stays high when eyes are open.

03 — Decide

Trigger the alert

A quick dip counts as a blink. If the ratio stays low past your alert delay, that's scored as a drowsy event and an audible alert fires immediately.

What is the eye aspect ratio?

Each eye is marked by six points: two at the corners, and two pairs along the top and bottom lid. When your eyes are open, the top and bottom points sit far apart. When they close, that vertical distance collapses toward zero while the corner-to-corner width barely changes.

Dividing the vertical distances by the width gives a ratio that's high and steady while you're alert, and falls fast the moment your eyes shut — which makes it a reliable, lighting-independent signal for fatigue.

Camera not working? Browsers only allow camera access on secure pages — https:// or http://localhost. Opening this file straight from disk (a file:// address) blocks it entirely. To run it locally:
  1. Open a terminal in the folder containing this file.
  2. Run python3 -m http.server 8000 (Python) or npx serve (Node).
  3. Visit http://localhost:8000/drowsiness-detection.html in your browser.
  4. Allow the camera permission prompt when it appears.
